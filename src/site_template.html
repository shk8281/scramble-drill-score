By position

Running backs start scrambles the most open, often as checkdowns, and lose the most ground as defenders rally. Tight ends are the least likely to create new separation.

Takeaways

For coaches

Drill scramble rules. A receiver who works for one more yard after the QB breaks the pocket turns a coin-flip throw into a likely completion.

For broadcasters

"Separation gained on the scramble" is a simple on-screen stat. Replay a play with the live gap line to show viewers who kept working.

For scouts

The leaderboard separates receivers who improvise well from those who stall. Pair it with film, since samples are 12 to 27 snaps per player.

Method and limits

  1. Scramble plays: PFF dropBackType contains "SCRAMBLE" and the pass was complete, incomplete or intercepted. Designed rollouts are excluded.
  2. Scramble start: first frame after the snap where the QB is more than 3 yards sideways from his snap position.
  3. Separation: distance from each route runner to the nearest defender in coverage, at scramble start and at the throw.
  4. Target: parsed from the play description and matched by last name to a route runner on that play. Throwaways are excluded.
  5. Outcome: completion from plays.csv. Interceptions count as incompletions.
  • Association, not causation. The QB may already be looking at the receiver who is working.
  • Tracking ends 0.5 s after the throw, so outcomes come from plays.csv.
  • The 3-yard sideways rule is an estimate of when the scramble starts.
  • Eight weeks of data. The team-wide result is solid; individual player numbers are noisy.
Data: NFL Big Data Bowl regional event data (source), NFL Next Gen Stats tracking and PFF scouting, 2021 weeks 1-8. Built with Kiro. Code and full write-up are in this repository's README.
